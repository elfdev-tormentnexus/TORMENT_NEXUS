# Reduce smoke exposure

Wildfire smoke can be harmful even when it is hard to see or smell. Check the
current Air Quality Health Index, air-quality alerts, and local instructions;
this offline copy cannot report them.

- Reduce or reschedule strenuous outdoor activity when smoke affects the area,
  especially for people at higher risk or anyone developing symptoms.
- Keep windows and doors closed when indoor temperature permits. Use the
  highest-quality filter the ventilation system can safely handle and a
  properly sized certified portable air cleaner when available.
- A well-fitting NIOSH-certified N95 or equivalent respirator can reduce
  exposure to fine particles outdoors, but it does not protect against smoke
  gases. It is unsuitable for a child under two, someone who cannot breathe
  comfortably in it, or anyone unable to remove it without help.
- When smoke and extreme heat occur together, staying cool takes priority over
  keeping every opening closed.

Serious breathing or cardiac symptoms need urgent medical attention. Cleaner
air locations, evacuation orders, workplace rules, and local smoke conditions
must come from current authorities.
