# Identify the hazard before choosing the action

Follow current hazard-specific instructions from local officials. This offline
card cannot determine whether sheltering or evacuation is safe for a live
incident.

Shelter-in-place usually means a danger is outside. Enter or remain in a safe
building, move away from exterior openings, monitor official information, and
stay until authorities change the instruction. For an outside airborne
chemical or radiological release, officials may direct people to close doors,
windows, and dampers; stop ventilation that draws outdoor air; and use an
interior room.

If hazardous material was released inside the building, leaving may instead
be the correct action. Shelter-in-place is also not the same as a lockdown,
tornado shelter, ordinary fire evacuation, or wildfire evacuation.

Do not take potassium iodide unless public-health authorities specifically
direct it; it helps only in particular radiological situations and can cause
harm when used incorrectly. Building-specific ventilation and evacuation
procedures, contamination boundaries, and current official orders always
override this general summary.
