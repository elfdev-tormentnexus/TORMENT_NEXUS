# Food

Keep cold-storage doors closed during an outage and use thermometers when
available. Food that looks or smells normal can still be unsafe. Do not taste
food to test it. Discard items that contacted floodwater, leaked chemicals, or
damaged containers, and follow current Health Canada or local public-health
directions after an outage or contamination event.

Keep raw meat and its juices away from ready-to-eat food. Clean and disinfect
surfaces contaminated during thawing. When official guidance, temperature,
and elapsed time do not establish safety, discard the food.

# Drinking water

Follow boil-water, do-not-consume, or other municipal advisories exactly:
different advisories require different responses. Do not assume that boiling
removes chemicals, fuel, or every contaminant. Use sealed water from a known
safe source when instructed, including for drinking, cooking, brushing teeth,
ice, infant formula, and pets as the advisory specifies.
