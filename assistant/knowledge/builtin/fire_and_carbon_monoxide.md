# If a fire or carbon-monoxide alarm sounds

Treat an alarm as real. Get everyone out, move to fresh air and a safe meeting
place, and contact local emergency services from outside. Do not search for
the carbon-monoxide source, re-enter for belongings, or return until emergency
personnel or a qualified professional says it is safe.

Carbon monoxide cannot be reliably seen, smelled, or tasted. Headache,
dizziness, nausea, weakness, confusion, chest pain, or unusual sleepiness can
be signs of exposure, but symptoms are not a detector.

## Prevention

- Install and test smoke and carbon-monoxide alarms in the locations required
  by local rules and the manufacturer.
- Keep exits clear and practise two ways out when possible.
- Never run vehicles, generators, grills, or other combustion engines in an
  attached garage or enclosed space.
- Have fuel-burning appliances and vents installed and serviced by qualified
  people.

This card cannot diagnose an alarm or exposure and must not delay evacuation.
