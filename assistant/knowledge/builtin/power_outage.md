# During a power outage

First determine whether anyone depends on powered medical, mobility, heating,
cooling, or communication equipment. Use the household emergency plan and
contact the relevant service or local authorities early if backup power is
insufficient.

- Use flashlights rather than unattended flames.
- Keep generators, grills, camp stoves, and fuel-burning equipment outdoors
  and well away from doors, windows, and vents. Never use them in a home,
  garage, shed, or other enclosed space.
- Unplug sensitive equipment if the supply is unstable. Leave one light on so
  restoration is visible.
- Treat fallen lines and damaged electrical equipment as live. Keep away and
  report them to the utility or local authorities.
- Use a generator only according to its manual and a properly installed
  transfer arrangement. Never feed a home circuit through a wall outlet.

## Refrigerated food

Keep refrigerator and freezer doors closed. Health Canada says an unopened
refrigerator generally stays cold for about four hours, a full freezer for
about 48 hours, and a half-full freezer for about 24 hours. Time is only a
guide: use appliance thermometers and current official food-safety advice.
Discard food when its safety is uncertain; smell and appearance cannot prove
that food is safe.
