# Get to heated shelter early

Numbness, loss of coordination, confusion, unusual drowsiness, slurred speech,
or severe shivering can signal dangerous cold injury. Move to shelter, replace
wet clothing with dry layers, and seek urgent medical help for serious
hypothermia or frostbite. Handle an unconscious or severely cold person gently.

# Reduce cold exposure

- Check current weather and wind-chill alerts before travel or outdoor work.
- Wear dry layers, a wind-resistant outer layer, insulated waterproof
  footwear, mittens, and head and face protection.
- Change wet clothing promptly. Do not rub or massage frostbitten skin.
- Avoid alcohol before cold exposure. A health condition or medication can
  change personal risk; ask a health professional.
- Avoid strenuous snow clearing when a heart or other medical condition makes
  exertion risky.

No fixed exposure time is safe for everyone. Wind, moisture, clothing,
activity, age, and health all change the risk. Warming-centre locations,
travel restrictions, and local cold warnings require a current source.
