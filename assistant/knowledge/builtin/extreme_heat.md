# Suspected heat stroke is an emergency

Call 9-1-1 or the local emergency number immediately when a person in the heat
has a high body temperature with confusion, poor coordination, fainting,
unconsciousness, or very hot or red skin. While help is coming, move them to a
cooler place when possible and cool large areas of skin or clothing with cold
water.

# Reduce heat exposure

- Use an air-conditioned or shaded place, slow down, take extra breaks, and
  drink water before thirst becomes strong.
- Check on people who may need help, preferably in person during very hot
  weather.
- Block direct sun from windows. Open windows only when outdoor air is cooler
  and current air-quality advice permits it.
- A fan can improve comfort but does not cool the body when the surrounding
  temperature is above 35°C; move to a cooler place instead.
- Never leave a person or animal in a parked vehicle.

Medication, health-condition, cooling-centre, and weather-alert advice must
come from a clinician or a current local source. If extreme heat and wildfire
smoke happen together, preventing dangerous overheating takes priority.

Related terms: heat warning, heat wave, heat exhaustion, heat stroke,
dehydration, cooling centre, hot apartment, fan, air conditioning.
