# Stop and verify

Offline manuals are useful when the network is unavailable, but a retrieved
passage is not proof that it is current, complete, applicable, or correctly
interpreted. Check a current authoritative source or qualified person before
acting when the answer depends on:

- an active emergency, symptoms, dosage, diagnosis, or treatment;
- law, regulation, permits, radio rules, taxes, benefits, or deadlines;
- electrical, gas, structural, vehicle, firearm, climbing, diving, or other
  safety-critical work;
- a specific product, chemical, battery, connector, torque, polarity, wiring
  diagram, firmware version, or recall;
- weather, roads, evacuation orders, prices, schedules, security advisories,
  or any fact that changes with time.

If a manual conflicts with a label, current official direction, the operator's
new evidence, or the exact model/serial/edition in front of them, stop. Identify
the conflict rather than blending the sources into a confident answer.

Retrieval scores only rank text. They do not measure truth, safety, authority,
or whether a passage applies to the present situation.

Related terms: verify, fact check, outdated manual, stale information, safety
critical, professional advice, current information, wrong edition.
